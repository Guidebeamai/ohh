Open specification. v0.2 released, v0.3 in design.

Publish what you do once. Share the rest only when you approve.

The Open Hiring Harness is an open standard for a professional profile you own. Platforms, recruiters and AI assistants read the public part directly. Your rates, availability and references stay sealed until you approve a specific request.

Why it exists

Your working life is scattered across platforms that own it, and the parts that matter most are either locked away or leaked.

  • You rebuild yourself everywhere

    Every marketplace, job board and recruiting tool wants the same profile in its own format, and keeps your reputation when you leave.

  • Private details travel without you

    Once rates, references or availability are in a profile, you lose track of who has read them and why.

  • Claims all look the same

    "Expert in Spark" reads the same whether you typed it, a test measured it or an employer vouched for it. With AI writing every CV, that difference is the whole signal.

How access works

A harness has two parts: a public file anyone can read, and protected sections that are released one request at a time. In v0.3 a request works like this.

  1. A platform asks, and says why

    A recruiter's platform requests your rates with a stated purpose: Evaluate fit for a three-month data pipeline build.

  2. You see who is really asking

    The request shows the platform's verified domain, not just the name it gives itself, along with its purpose and the sections it wants.

  3. You approve, or your rules do

    Rules you set can approve routine requests for you. Private sections always ask you directly.

  4. They get access to that section, for a limited time

    The key works only for the sections you approved, only for your harness, and only until it expires. Revoke it and it stops working within five minutes.

This is standard OAuth, using the UMA 2.0 grant designed for one person approving another's access. OHH adds the vocabulary, not a new security protocol. Every approval, refusal and revocation is recorded for you, and that record is never published.

Every claim says where it came from

From v0.3, each skill, reference and result records its source. The standard labels the evidence. It never turns it into a score.

Source What it means Example
self_declared You said so "Python: expert", typed by you
assessed A named, validated assessment measured it An interest profile from a published instrument
first_party Recorded by the system that saw it happen A course completion from the provider's records
attested A named third party vouches for it An employer's report at the end of a placement
inferred Worked out by software, including AI Skills an AI extracted from a CV

Who it's for

v0.2 assumed every harness belonged to a freelancer. v0.3 doesn't.

People

  • Independents. Freelancers, contractors and consultants who quote rates and take engagements.
  • Job seekers. One record of roles you want and evidence of what you can do, readable by any platform.
  • Employees. A portable record that stays yours when you change jobs.
  • Students, from v0.3.1. With stricter rules: no public file by default, never indexed, and a guardian approves alongside the student.

Builders

  • Platforms and recruiting tools. Read a standard profile instead of scraping, and ask for more through one consent flow.
  • AI assistants. Read the public file, request protected sections like any other requester, or connect over MCP as the owner's own app.
  • Hosts. Schools, career services and employers can host harnesses for many people, one address each.

Roadmap

Breaking changes are proposed in the open and stay open for comment for at least 14 days.

  1. v0.2

    The format and how to find it Current

    Schema, discovery at a well-known address, and a consent model on paper. Can't keep sections private.

  2. v0.3

    Protected access, provenance and profile kinds In design

    Public and protected parts, access through OAuth, a source on every claim, and room for job seekers and employees. Read the proposal

  3. v0.3.1

    Students and guardian consent Planned

    Harnesses for people in school, with no public file by default and approval from a guardian as well as the student.

  4. v0.4

    Claims anyone can check Planned

    References and results issued as W3C Verifiable Credentials, building on Open Badges 3.0, so an attested claim can be verified rather than trusted.

  5. Later

    AI agents as hireable entities Proposal

    Agents that work on someone's behalf, or offer services themselves, under the same consent rules. Read the proposal

Principles

  • You're the source of truth

    Platforms read your harness under your rules. They don't own it.

  • Private means private

    Nothing leaves the protected part without an approved, time-limited, revocable request.

  • Evidence, not scores

    The standard says where a claim came from. It never ranks people.

  • Existing standards first

    OAuth for access, Verifiable Credentials for proof, standard occupation codes for skills. No home-made security.

  • Anyone can implement it

    Nothing in the specification needs Guidebeam's software or accounts.

  • People aren't inventory

    No engagement tricks, no attention capture, no selling access to people.
